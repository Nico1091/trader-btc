[1] CENTRAL BANKS A central bank is a financial institution that is responsible for  formulating and overseeing  the monetary system and policy of a nation or group of nations, regulating its money supply,  regulating member banks and  setting interest rates. Central  banks control and manipulate the national money  supply by their actions and policies. Central banks regulate member banks through a number of tools like capital requirements, reserve requirements. They also provide loans for the banks, and they manage foreign exchange reserves. The Central bank can serve as an emergency lender to financially troubled institutions, commercial banks and even the government. Major responsibilities of central banks
[2] MAJOR CENTRAL BANKS IN THE WORLD The Federal reserve bank also know as FED is the central bank of the United States of America which consists of 12 regional reserve banks. The monetary policy making body for the FED is called the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC). The FOMC consists of 12 members, which are: 7 members of the board of governors and 5 of the 12 reserve bank presidents. The FED aims to keep inflation at about 2% THE FEDERAL RESERVE BANK (USA) The European Central Bank also know as the ECB is the central bank for the Eurozone. It manages the Euro and frames and implements the EU economic and monetary policies. The monetary policy making body for the ECB is called the Governing Council. The Governing Council consists of 6 members of the Executive board, and the Governors of the National Central Banks of the 20 Eurozone countries. The ECB aims to keep inflation at about 2% THE EUROPEAN CENTRAL BANK (EUROPE)

[6] HOW CENTRAL BANKS ACHIEVE ECONOMIC STABILITY Interest rates are simply the value charged by central banks for lending money to private banks. They are a primary tool used to regulate  inflation.  INTEREST RATES Central banks affect the quantity of money in circulation by buying or selling  government securities  through the process known  as open market operations (OMO). OPEN MARKET OPERATIONS RESERVE REQUIREMENTS QUANTITATIVE EASING Central  banks can increase the amount of money in circulation by simply printing it. They can print as much money as they want, though there are consequences for doing so.  MONEY PRINTING Central  banks mandate commercial banks to keep a certain amount of funds in reserve (stored in vaults or at the central bank) against the amount of deposits in their clients' accounts. Under  quantitative easing,  central banks create money and use it to buy up assets and securities such as government bonds . This usually happen in dire economic times.
