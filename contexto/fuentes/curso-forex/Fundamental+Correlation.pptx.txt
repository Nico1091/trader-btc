[1] FUNDAMENTAL CORRELATION Fundamental correlation describes the relationship between two economic indicators. It can be described as either strong or weak, and as either positive or negative. Correlation is not always absolute (perfect positive or perfect negative) but in certain degrees of positivity or negativity, so it’s always advisable to use multiple forms of analysis to draw investment conclusions, and not just depend on a single correlation theory.  A perfect positive correlation means that if one economic data comes out, either as positive or negative, the other one will also come out in the same direction as it. A perfect negative correlation implies the direct opposite of a perfect positive correlation. Fundamental analysts are able to predict the outcomes  of economic  data releases by considering multiple forms of analysis, including fundamental correlations. Analysts will usually use the past outcomes of certain economic reports, to predict the outcome of a major news release and therefore trade it.
